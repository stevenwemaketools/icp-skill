# The method: Best Customer First

The skill runs one procedure with two entry paths. Read this file once per run, then follow the slot checklists in
`SKILL.md`. Every threshold the skill applies is written here so the synthesis is a judgment call on evidence, not a
guess.

Attribution: the spine is Alex Hormozi's "Your First Avatar" ($100M Lost Chapters) and his market criteria from
$100M Offers. The hardening comes from the B2B canon: closed-won analysis, Lincoln Murphy's situational ICP,
Bill Aulet's beachhead market, Chet Holmes's best buyers, April Dunford's positioning. Quote sparingly, with the
author's name, and only where the reader benefits.

## 1. Why the customer comes before the offer

Hormozi's order: market, then offer, then persuasion. "We are not trying to create demand. We are trying to channel
it." A business that serves everyone competes on price with everyone. A business that names its customer can state
requirements up front, which "repels the bad customers and attracts the good ones."

The money logic (Hormozi): compare lifetime gross profit (LTGP) with the cost to acquire a customer (CAC). Best
customers pay more and stay longer, so their LTGP is a multiple of an average customer's. He would "rather pay
$5,000 to acquire $45,000 than $1,000 to acquire $5,000." Floor: LTGP at least 3 times CAC. Every time he removed
qualification steps, "lead volume increased, but we made less money."

## 2. Path A: the lead has customers (10 or more paying customers)

Hormozi's four steps, adapted to a conversation instead of a survey form.

1. **Harvest.** Ask for the best 5 to 10 customers (names or aliases) and the 2 to 3 worst. For the best, collect
   the four groups of facts he surveys:
   - demographics and firmographics: industry, size, role of the buyer, location, stage, ticket, years in business;
   - business stats before and now: what changed after they bought;
   - aspirations: the goal they had when they bought and the problem they were solving;
   - buying process: the single best reason they bought, the trigger event, where they first saw the business, what
     they consumed before buying, whether someone referred them, how long from first contact to purchase.
2. **Rank.** Sort by "the customers you like the most, who spend the most and stay the longest." Score each on five
   signals: pays the most, stays the longest, gets the best results, is the easiest to serve, refers others. Keep the
   top 20 percent. With 10 customers that is the top 2; with 40, the top 8. Mark the bottom 20 percent as well.
3. **Qualifiers.** Find "the fewest qualifiers that they all have in common", usually 3 to 5. Lay them across five
   layers so none is missed:
   - firmographic (industry, size, geography, stage);
   - technographic (tools they already use or pay for);
   - situational (the trigger event: a hire, a launch, a failed vendor, a new location, a regulation);
   - readiness (a person who owns the problem, a budget, a number they track);
   - aspiration and psychographic (what they are trying to become, what they refuse to accept).
   **Separation test:** the bottom 20 percent must NOT match the qualifiers. If they do, add a qualifier until the
   two groups separate. A profile that fits everyone is not a profile. Target: the qualifiers exclude at least 70
   percent of the businesses in the lead's market.
4. **Execute.** State the requirements up front in every message; speak only to that avatar; push harder on the
   channels the best customers came through; reverse-engineer the buying path they took and make it happen on
   purpose (Hormozi's gym business found 78 percent of its best customers had consumed two or more long-form
   pieces before buying, so it added two to every lead's journey); stop selling to anyone outside the profile.

## 3. Path B: the lead has no customers yet (or fewer than 10, or a new line)

Hormozi: start with what you know. Pick a niche you have experience in, serve a narrow target first, and rerun
Path A once there are customers. Aulet: pick one beachhead and win it before expanding.

1. **What you know.** Pain the lead has lived, profession they are paid for, passion they will not quit, people
   they already know. Business is "solving problems that exist and making sure the math makes sense": where is the
   problem, how much do they think it is a problem, can you solve it and charge for it.
2. **Candidates.** 3 to 5 segments. Propose them if the lead is blank.
3. **Scorecard.** Score each on the four market indicators (section 4) and on Aulet's beachhead criteria
   (`market-path.md`). Pick the highest total unless the lead has a strong reason, and write the reason down.
4. **Hypothesis avatar.** 3 to 5 qualifiers, a trigger, the words they use for the pain, anti-ICP.
5. **Next 10 customers.** Name 5 to 10 real or realistic first customers and plan inquiry-mode interviews: ask and
   listen, do not sell. The profile is confirmed when those 10 fit the requirements and buy faster.
Harvest the 1 to 9 existing customers as evidence inside Path B when there are some.

## 4. The stress test (both paths)

**Four market indicators** (Hormozi), scored 1 to 10 each:
- Pain: they do not want it, they desperately need it. Test: what gets worse in 12 months if they do nothing?
- Purchasing power: they can pay the price without a loan or a committee.
- Easy to target: identifiable, reachable, congregated. Test: could the lead list 100 of them in a week?
- Growing: more of them next year than this year.
Bands for the total out of 40 (our operationalization, not Hormozi's): 32 to 40 strong market, 24 to 31 attractive,
16 to 23 difficult, 15 or less weak. Below 24, narrow or switch before building anything.

**Ready, willing, able** (Lincoln Murphy): ready = the problem exists and feels urgent; willing = they are acting on
it, usually after a catalyst; able = budget, authority, and a buying process the lead can survive.

**They talk to each other** (Aulet): a valid segment buys similar things, has similar sales cycles, and shares
references. Word of mouth only works inside such a segment.

**Value equation** (Hormozi): dream outcome times perceived likelihood, divided by time delay times effort. The
right avatar is the one for whom the lead's offer scores highest on all four. If the dream outcome is small or the
lead cannot make it believable for this avatar, the avatar is wrong, not the offer.

**Niche ladder** (Hormozi's pricing ladder): narrow one descriptor at a time. Generic "time management" sold for
$19; "for sales professionals" for $99; "for B2B outbound sales reps" for $499; "for B2B outbound power tools and
gardening sales reps" for $1,000 to $2,000. Stop one rung before the segment stops being reachable (the 100-in-a-week
test fails) or stops being big enough to hit the lead's goal. Then commit: "once you pick a market, commit until
you win."

## 5. The North Star sentence

One sentence, 220 characters or fewer, no dash punctuation. Formula:

> Vendo [la oferta] a [3 a 5 calificadores] que [dolor o aspiración], cuando [el trigger].
> I sell [the offer] to [3 to 5 qualifiers] who [pain or aspiration], when [the trigger].

Checks: a stranger could decide in 10 seconds whether they are in or out; the qualifiers are observable (size,
volume, role, tool, location), not adjectives ("ambitious", "serious"); the trigger names a moment, not a mood.

## 6. The requirements and the anti-ICP

Requirements (3 to 5) are the qualifiers written as conditions the lead will state publicly, Gym Launch style: "a
signed lease, more than one employee, $10k or more a month in revenue, at least 30 customers." Each has a one-line
why. The anti-ICP (3 to 6) names who the lead will decline and why; it should read as a relief, not an insult.
"Decir que no es parte del perfil."

## 7. Where they are and how they buy

Watering holes: communities, events, allies (people who already sell to the avatar), channels. Buying path: the
sequence the best customers actually followed (content consumed, the referral, the first call, the trial), so the
30-day plan can rebuild it on purpose. Dream 100 (Chet Holmes): a named list of the accounts that would matter
most; in his magazine story 167 of 2,200 companies bought 95 percent of the ad pages. Seed it with the names the
lead already knows.

## 8. The 30-day plan and metrics

Four weeks, two to three actions each, in this order: rewrite every message around the requirements; list 100 and
reach out; rebuild the buying path and stop selling outside the profile; measure and re-score. Default metrics
unless the lead has better ones: LTGP to CAC at 3 to 1 or better; close rate 30 percent or better (below that the
avatar or the sales process is wrong); retention at 90 days equal or better than the best customers'. Review the
profile every quarter and rerun "fire the worst, double down on the best" twice a year.

## 9. The icp.json contract

`scripts/make_pdf.py` validates and renders this shape. Copy the structure of `assets/sample_icp.es.json` (Path A)
or `assets/sample_icp.en.json` (Path B); the samples are fictional.

Required: `schema_version` "1.0" · `lang` es|en · `generated_at` ISO date · `path` A|B ·
`business{name, offer}` · `north_star` (one sentence, 220 chars max) · `profile{label, requirements[3..5]{name, why}}` ·
`evidence{basis, market_scores{pain, purchasing_power, easy_to_target, growing} (integers 1 to 10)}` ·
`anti_icp[3..6]{who, why}` · `where{watering_holes[3+]{place, type}, buying_path[3+]{step, note?}}` ·
`plan_30_days[4]{week, actions[]}`.
Path A also requires `evidence.separation_test{passed (bool), note}`. Path B also requires
`evidence.segments_considered[3+]{name, scores{...}, total, picked, note?}` with exactly one `picked: true`, and
`next_10_customers[5+]`.
Optional: `business{owner, geography, customers_count}` · `profile{demographics{industry, size, role, location,
stage, ticket}, situation_before, situation_after, aspirations[], pain_language[]}` · `evidence{top_customers[]{alias,
why}, rwa{ready, willing, able}, talk_to_each_other, list_100_in_a_week, niche_ladder[]{level, note}}` ·
`where.dream_100_seed[]` · `metrics[]{name, target}` (defaults injected when empty) · `open_questions[]` (every
"no sé" ends here and prints under "Por confirmar") · `cta{override: null}`.

Write numbers only when the lead gave them. Anything assumed goes to `open_questions` and is labelled as an
assumption in the chat.
