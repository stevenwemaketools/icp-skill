# Two worked examples (fictional)

Both correspond to the sample files in `assets/`. Read them to see what a finished run looks like; do not reuse
their facts for a real lead.

## Example A, Path A: Ruta Clara Logística (`assets/sample_icp.es.json`)

Last-mile delivery with live tracking for e-commerce brands in Santo Domingo, 40 customers. Carolina (the owner)
named 8 customers she would clone and 3 she would not. The best pay the top plan, stay more than 12 months and
refer other brands; all of them ship more than 300 orders a month, sell direct to consumers in the city, have one
person in charge of operations, and had already paid another courier and been disappointed. The worst ship fewer
than 120 orders and have nobody in operations: the separation test passes with the four qualifiers alone.

Market indicators: pain 9, purchasing power 7, easy to target 8, growing 8 (total 32, strong). The niche ladder
goes from "businesses that ship parcels" to "e-commerce brands in Santo Domingo" to "DTC brands with 300 or more
orders a month and active ads", which is still about 400 brands in the city, so reachable. Buying path: two reels
about failed deliveries, a DM with a failed-delivery cost calculator, a 20-minute call with the operations manager,
a two-week trial with 50 orders.

North Star: "Vendo entregas de última milla con rastreo en vivo a marcas de e-commerce en Santo Domingo que
despachan más de 300 pedidos al mes y pierden clientes por entregas fallidas, justo cuando cambian de courier."

The 30-day plan rewrites the bio, landing and DMs around the four requirements, lists 100 matching brands, asks
each of the 8 best customers for two referrals ("quién, no si"), makes the two-week trial a fixed sales step, stops
quoting stores under 100 orders, and measures close rate, CAC and failed deliveries per new customer.

## Example B, Path B: Ledger Dental (`assets/sample_icp.en.json`)

Marcus wants to sell monthly bookkeeping with cash-flow reports and has no customers. What he knows: eight years as
a controller for a dental group, and a CPA friend who refers him clients. Four candidate segments were scored:
independent dental clinics with 2 to 5 dentists (29/40), veterinary clinics (26), physiotherapy practices (25), med
spas (25). Dental wins on purchasing power and on the clarity of the trigger events (a bookkeeper leaves, a second
location opens, the CPA flags late books).

Hypothesis North Star: "I sell monthly bookkeeping with cash-flow reports to independent dental clinics in Texas
with 2 to 5 dentists that just lost or outgrew their bookkeeper."

Requirements: 2 to 5 dentists under one owner; independent, not part of a DSO; a trigger in the last 90 days.
Anti-ICP: DSO-owned clinics, first-year solo dentists, anyone who only wants tax filing. Watering holes: the state
dental association meetings, practice brokers and lenders, office-manager Facebook groups, supply reps. The plan
interviews 10 office managers in inquiry mode, meets brokers and supply reps, offers a 15-minute books check to 30
clinics, and closes the first three on a first-month-free offer. Two open questions print under "To confirm".
